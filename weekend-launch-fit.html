Is Weekend Launch Right for You?

This strategy maximizes net proceeds in the right conditions — and can cost a seller money in the wrong ones. Here's an honest look at both.

Weekend Launch works by manufacturing real competition — but it only works if there's genuine buyer demand underneath it to reveal. It doesn't create demand out of nothing. Before recommending this strategy, walk through both sides below with your seller.
✅ Good Fit
Genuine Seller's MarketReal underlying demand for the strategy to concentrate and reveal — low inventory, multiple showings already expected.
Move-In ReadyNo major deferred maintenance likely to stall a buyer mid-decision during the compressed window.
Broadly Appealing Price BandStandard suburban single-family product with a deep, active buyer pool at this price point.
Optimizing for Net, Not SpeedSeller can tolerate a 10–14 day window because the goal is the highest true number, not the fastest close.
⚠️ Not a Good Fit
Soft or Balanced MarketUnderpricing without real competitive pressure behind it is just underpricing — nothing bids it back up.
Unique or Distressed PropertyAcreage, highly custom homes, or anything needing real repair have thin buyer pools regardless of list price.
Unusual Price PointIf there's no meaningfully larger buyer tier just below your home's value, there's no extra pool to unlock.
Seller Needs Guaranteed SpeedAlready under contract elsewhere or on a hard relocation deadline — can't afford the two-weekend wait even if it nets more.

How We Actually Set the Price

There's no fixed percentage rule — "6% under" or "10% under" isn't the point. What matters is where the real round-number search thresholds sit relative to your home's true value: $450K, $475K, $500K, $550K. We price just under whichever threshold meaningfully expands your buyer pool, without going so far under that a sophisticated buyer's agent starts wondering what's wrong with the house.

Sometimes that's 6% under. Sometimes it's closer to 9–10%, especially when a threshold like $450K lines up with common buyer financing caps, not just a search filter. Every listing gets its own number — not a template.
